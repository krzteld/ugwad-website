Traditional Buri Weaving of Anini-y

Discover how native buri palm fronds become sustainable livelihood crafts in Brgy. San Francisco, Anini-y, Antique.

Local Kinaray-a & Weaving Terms

  • Ugwad: A Kinaray-a word meaning "to progress, rise, or prosper"—representing our women weavers' mission to uplift their families through local craftsmanship.
  • Buri (Corypha utan): A native Philippine palm tree whose large fan-shaped leaves are harvested sustainably without cutting down the tree.
  • Banig: A traditional handwoven sleeping or floor mat prized for its coolness and durability.
  • Smoked Buri: A natural smoking technique used by Anini-y artisans to give raw buri strips a rich, earthy golden-brown hue without synthetic chemicals.

From Palm Leaf to Finished Craft: 5 Steps

1. Harvesting Young Fronds

Weavers selectively harvest unopened young leaves (ubod) from mature buri palms in Anini-y, ensuring the palm continues to grow.

2. Stripping & Sun-Drying

The leaf blades are separated from the midribs and dried under the Antique sun until they turn supple and pale cream.

3. Natural Smoking or Dyeing

Strips are either naturally smoked over coconut husks for warm earth tones or boiled in eco-friendly dyes for vibrant accents.

4. Flattening & Sizing

Artisans smooth each strip using a bamboo blade and slice them into uniform widths depending on whether they are weaving a fine bag or a sturdy banig.

5. Handweaving & Finishing

Using traditional over-and-under plaiting (lala), weavers craft bags, placemats, and mats by hand—taking anywhere from two days to two weeks per piece.
